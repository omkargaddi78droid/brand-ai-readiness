Choosing a bike lock is one of those decisions that only feels urgent after a bike has already gone missing from a rack outside a coffee shop or a train station. The Harbor Bike Lock has built a reputation among urban commuters for being heavy enough to deter casual theft without being so bulky it becomes annoying to carry on a daily commute. Most buying guides in this category start by explaining the difference between cable locks, U-locks, and chain locks, since each style trades weight against resistance differently depending on where a bike is usually parked. A cable lock is light and easy to coil into a bag but offers the least resistance against a determined thief with the right tools. A U-lock strikes a middle ground that many urban riders settle on after replacing a stolen bike once already in a busy city. Chain locks tend to be the heaviest option but can wrap around awkward shapes like a parking meter or an oddly placed bike rack post that a rigid U-lock cannot reach around. Riders locking a bike outside overnight are generally advised to prioritize resistance over weight, since a determined thief has far more time to work uninterrupted after dark. Locking through the frame and at least one wheel is a frequently repeated piece of advice throughout this guide, since a wheel alone is often not worth stealing on its own.
